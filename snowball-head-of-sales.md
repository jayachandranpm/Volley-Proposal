# Challenge 2 · Head of Sales for Snowball Agency

> Three real candidates. Each picked against a specific thesis about who can actually close Snowball's six-figure brand-side engagement deal — not the obvious org-chart move.

---

## What Snowball is actually hiring for

Before naming anyone, I tried to define the deal. Most "Head of Sales" hires fail because the company writes the JD around the title and not the motion.

**What Snowball sells (from snowball.agency + Ryan Hashemi's bio):**
- A **12-month consultative engagement** that builds the brand's *internal* YouTube content competency — they train, ideate, create, scale, then "get out the way"
- The portfolio is the proof: Vogue *73 Questions* (500M views), Wired *Auto Complete* (200M), Jubilee *Odd Man Out* (300M), Mini Katana (11B), GQ *10 Essentials* — i.e. **iconic series that drove $300M+ in revenue and 250+ successful hires**
- The buyer is a **brand-side decision maker** (Head of Brand / VP Content / CMO) at a Fortune 1000 or a YouTube-native DTC brand
- Average deal size is six-to-seven figures and the cycle is multi-month
- Founder-led, small team, no SDR factory to inherit

**So the Head of Sales must be three things at once:**
1. **Has personally closed six-figure consultative deals to brand-side marketing leaders** — not media buys, not SaaS subscriptions
2. **Earned credibility in the creator economy** — they can talk CTR, watch time, and YouTube algo with a CMO without losing the room
3. **Comfortable being deal #1, not employee #150** — player-coach, not org-chart manager

**Where I refused to hunt:** SaaS VPs (HubSpot/Gong — wrong motion), holding-co account directors (Publicis/WPP — they sell what's on the menu, not pipeline), and YouTube/Google partnerships (Ryan already knows them). Obvious org-chart picks waste the brief.

**Where I did hunt:** People who already sell *exactly this product* at a competitor, founder-operators who've built the same engine on the brand side, and sales-led founders whose pitch language is already deal-shaped.

## How I'd run the outreach

Three notes the brief didn't ask for but matter:

1. **Sequencing.** Send to Candidate 3 first (lowest-probability yes, highest-signal pitch sharpener). If he says no, ask him who he'd hire — founders love giving advice. Use that intel to refine the DM going to Candidates 1 and 2.
2. **Warming.** All three get one genuine, specific comment on a recent post 48 hours before the DM. Cold DMs convert at ~3%; warm DMs convert at ~15%. Don't say "great post" — pick one specific line and disagree with it slightly.
3. **The real test.** If none of the three reply, the problem isn't the candidates — it's that Snowball's written equity/comp story isn't sharp enough to outweigh "stay where you are." That's the conversation I'd have with Ryan before sending DM #4.
