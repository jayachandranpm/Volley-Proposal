Challenge 02 · Snowball
Head of Sales hunt

Three real candidates.
Three different bets.

Each one picked against a specific thesis about who can actually close Snowball's six-figure brand-side engagement deal — not the obvious org-chart move.

What Snowball is actually hiring for

Before naming anyone, I tried to define the deal. Most "Head of Sales" hires fail because the company writes the JD around the title and not the motion.

What Snowball sells

  • A 12-month consultative engagement that builds the brand's internal YouTube content competency — train, ideate, create, scale, then "get out the way"
  • The portfolio is the proof: Vogue 73 Questions (500M views), Wired Auto Complete (200M), Jubilee Odd Man Out (300M), Mini Katana (11B), GQ 10 Essentials — iconic series that drove $300M+ in revenue and 250+ successful hires
  • The buyer is a brand-side decision maker (Head of Brand / VP Content / CMO) at a Fortune 1000 or a YouTube-native DTC brand
  • Average deal size is six-to-seven figures and the cycle is multi-month
  • Founder-led, small team, no SDR factory to inherit

So the Head of Sales must be three things at once

  • Has personally closed six-figure consultative deals to brand-side marketing leaders — not media buys, not SaaS subscriptions
  • Earned credibility in the creator economy — they can talk CTR, watch time, and YouTube algo with a CMO without losing the room
  • Comfortable being deal #1, not employee #150 — player-coach, not org-chart manager
Where I refused to hunt SaaS VPs (HubSpot/Gong — wrong motion), holding-co account directors (Publicis/WPP — they sell what's on the menu, not pipeline), and YouTube/Google partnerships people (Ryan already knows them all). Obvious org-chart picks waste the brief.
Where I did hunt People who already sell exactly this product at a competitor, founder-operators who've built the same engine on the brand side, and sales-led founders whose pitch language is already deal-shaped.
Pick 01 · The direct competitor's operator
Lara Della Gaspera

Current role: Business Lead, Whalar DACH (Berlin, Germany)

Past: Senior Creator Operations Manager at TikTok

"Business Lead @Whalar DACH / Creator economy, Media & Entertainment"

Why she'd crush it

Whalar is the closest commercial twin to Snowball — a creator-economy shop that sells multi-month strategic engagements to brand teams (not media buys). As DACH Business Lead she already runs P&L, owns deal-cycle, and pitches the same buyer Ryan needs to reach — a brand marketing leader who has to justify creator-economy spend to a CFO. Her TikTok background means she's lived inside the platform side of the deal, which is exactly the credibility you need to walk into a Vogue or GQ meeting and not get caught flat on a watch-time question. She's also currently running a region not a global function, which means she's hireable at the right altitude for an early-stage Snowball — a true rebuild, with founding-team upside.

Most VP Sales candidates have never sat in that conversation.
Ryan's hiring his first Head of Sales — global, founding-team, equity, no inherited org chart. I'd love 20 minutes to walk you through what he's building. Worst case you walk away with intel on how a category-defining shop pitches against Whalar.
Thursday or Friday work?

Why it lands. Names a real shared vocabulary in the subject line. Signals research depth (Whalar DACH motion ≠ generic "sales leader"). Reframes the move as a ladder up not sideways. Offers an asymmetric trade so a "no" still feels worth her 20 minutes.

Current role: Founder & CEO, YCA — Yama Creator Agency (formerly Ad Society) · Orange County, CA

"Talent Management | Influencer Marketing | Creator Economy | Social Media"

Why he'd crush it

Yama built a creator-talent agency from scratch and rebranded it (Ad Society → YCA) — which means he's been the person who closes every deal personally for years, eats the rejection, and has personally rebuilt the pitch when the market shifts. That's the exact muscle a founder-led shop needs in deal #1: not a salesperson who's been handed a quota, but an operator who knows how to walk into a brand-marketing meeting and convert it because his rent depends on it. He also has a mutual connection in our network (Mugil Vanjinath), so a warm intro path exists. He's not a household name — that's the point. Household-name VPs say no; founders rebuilding their thing say yes when the upside is right.

Asks a question instead of pitching — high reply rate. Explicitly de-positions the "career VP Sales" candidate so he reads himself into the role. Offers him the ego-out of "or just refer me to someone."
Pick 03 · The off-map sales-wired founder
rahul moudgil

Current role: Founder & CEO, Creator24 (US-based, building tools for creator managers)

"CEO @ Creator24 | we bring 3x more deals for managers"

Why he'd crush it

Look at his headline. It's not "passionate about the creator economy" or "building the future of." It's "we bring 3x more deals for managers." That single sentence tells you everything: this is a founder whose entire identity is wired around closing deals and proving ROI in numbers. Out of ~45 creator-economy candidates I scraped, his profile is the only one that talks in pure deal-shaped language. He's built and is currently selling a product to creator managers (his customers are sales-and-deal people), so he understands the deal-flow side of the creator economy not the platform/marketing side — which is the gap most candidates have. He also has a mutual connection in our network (Monika Celes), so a warm path exists.

The risk: he runs his own company, so the conversation is harder. The reward: if he says yes, you've hired the only candidate who'd close on day 30.

The DM I'd send

Subject: "3x more deals" — same pitch, bigger ticket
Rahul —
Out of the ~50 creator-economy founders I looked at this week, your headline is the only one that says "deals" instead of "passion." That's the reason this DM exists.
I work with Ryan Hashemi at Snowball — we're the team behind Vogue's 73 Questions, Wired's Auto Complete, Jubilee's Odd Man Out, Mini Katana (11B views). $300M+ revenue driven for our brand partners. Ryan's hiring his first Head of Sales and the brief is uncomfortably specific: it has to be someone who's already obsessed with deal mechanics, not someone who got promoted into selling.
I get that you're heads-down on Creator24. But two reasons to give me 20 minutes:
  1. The Snowball buyer (brand CMO) and the Creator24 buyer (creator manager) overlap more than you'd think — even if you say no, the conversation is a sales-channel scoping call for both of us
  2. If you ever sell or wind down Creator24, this is the most interesting next seat in the category — equity, founding-team, no SDR factory
Coffee or Zoom this week?
— [Your name]
Why it lands. Mirrors his own deal-language back at him in the first line (mirror neurons + ego). Acknowledges the "I have my own company" objection up front. Offers two distinct reasons to take the call so a "no on the job" doesn't kill the relationship. Closes with options, not a hard ask.

How I'd run the outreach

Three notes the brief didn't ask for but matter:

  • Sequencing. Send to Candidate 3 first (lowest-probability yes, highest-signal pitch sharpener). If he says no, ask him who he'd hire — founders love giving advice. Use that intel to refine the DM going to Candidates 1 and 2.
  • Warming. All three get one genuine, specific comment on a recent post 48 hours before the DM. Cold DMs convert at ~3%; warm DMs convert at ~15%. Don't say "great post" — pick one specific line and disagree with it slightly.
  • The real test. If none of the three reply, the problem isn't the candidates — it's that Snowball's written equity/comp story isn't sharp enough to outweigh "stay where you are." That's the conversation I'd have with Ryan before sending DM #4.
